# Source: https://www.agentweb.us/thesis

Every day, billions of hours of human attention are spent moving a cursor across a screen. We log in. We switch tabs. We re-enter the same address, the same card, the same details we have typed a thousand times before. We click through flows designed to be read by eyes and operated by hands. The internet is, quietly, one of the largest manual-labor systems ever built.

That made sense when people were the only ones using it. Pages were drawn for human vision. Buttons were sized for human fingers. Forms assumed a person would sit there, read the screen, and decide what to press next. The entire surface of the web is a user interface, and the user it was designed for was always a human being.

Now agents are arriving, and we are asking them to pretend to be us. We hand them a screenshot and tell them to find the button. We make them guess at pixels, replay clicks, and recover when the layout shifts by a few points. This is computer use, and it is slow, expensive, and fragile. It treats a machine like a person squinting at a monitor, when the machine never needed eyes at all.

Agents do not need visuals. They do not need to see a checkout page to pay, or read a calendar grid to book a time. They need the action itself: a clear, verified way to do the thing, with the right inputs and a record of what happened. The pixels were never the point. The pixels were a workaround for the fact that, until now, only humans were on the other end.

So the internet needs to change. Not by replacing the web people love, but by adding a second surface underneath it: one made of real, verified actions instead of screens to interpret. That is what we are building. AgentWeb gives any agent the right tools to complete a task on a real site, with your approval, reliably. Connect once, and delegate the work you used to do by hand. The web stops being something we operate, and starts being something that operates for us.
